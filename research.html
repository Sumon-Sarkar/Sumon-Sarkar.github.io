Research

My current and previous research experience spans three interconnected areas: the development of photocatalytic and radical methods for site-selective C–H functionalization; the application of machine learning to retrosynthetic planning; and data-driven approaches to asymmetric catalysis. These efforts are united by a commitment to solve experimental problems by developing synthetic and computational strategies. Computational tools are tested at the bench, and bench observations motivate the next generation of models.

1.Data-Driven Catalyst Design & Sustainable Synthesis

Catalyst and ligand optimization in asymmetric synthesis proceeds predominantly by empirical screening, which is resource-intensive and provides limited mechanistic insight into the origins of selectivity. At BIDMaP, with Prof. F. Dean Toste and Dr. Samuel Blau at LBNL, I am exploring data-driven approaches that connect catalyst and substrate structures to experimental selectivity. Stay tuned for further information.

2.AI-Assisted Retrosynthetic Planning

The procedural knowledge required for reliable synthesis planning, including the choice of reagents, solvent, temperature, stoichiometry, and workup, is distributed across millions of published reports and remains largely inaccessible to computational methods. We developed MOSAIC, a mixture-of-experts large language model framework in which 2,498 specialist models, each trained within a Voronoi-partitioned region of chemical reaction space, collectively generate complete and reproducible experimental protocols with associated confidence metrics.

Experimental validation across 35+ previously unreported compounds, spanning pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and functional materials, demonstrated a 71% overall success rate. Notably, MOSAIC proposed productive reaction conditions for transformations outside its direct training distribution, indicating capacity for meaningful extrapolation rather than mere interpolation.

Selected reference: Li, H.;# Sarkar, S.;# et al. Nature 2026, 651, 107.

3.Photocatalytic C–H Functionalization & Radical Methodology

Achieving site-selective functionalization of unactivated C(sp3)–H bonds remains a central challenge in synthesis. Visible-light excitation of transition-metal intermediates provides access to open-shell pathways unavailable under thermal conditions and can redirect elementary steps in conventional catalytic cycles toward new reactivity.

We demonstrated that photoexcitation of Pd–H species enhances hydride donor ability in the excited state, enabling altered hydropalladation and chemoselective head-to-tail alkene cross-dimerization. We further established that aryl triflates, conventionally limited to two-electron Pd(0)/Pd(II) oxidative addition, undergo single-electron reduction under visible light to generate aryl radicals competent for intramolecular C(sp3)–H arylation.

Finally, complementary metal-free strategies employing carbon-centered radicals and intramolecular HAT deliver site-selective α-, β-, and γ-C(sp3)–H borylation of aliphatic amines without transition metals.
