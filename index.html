I am a synthetic organic chemist working at the intersection of organic synthesis, catalysis, artificial intelligence, and computational chemistry. My research focuses on integrating machine learning, mechanistic understanding, and experimental synthesis to accelerate reaction discovery, catalyst design, and access to complex molecules.

Raised in West Bengal, India, I earned a B.Sc. in Chemistry from Jadavpur University and an M.Sc. from the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur. I began my Ph.D. with Prof. Vladimir Gevorgyan at the University of Illinois at Chicago and completed it at the University of Texas at Dallas. My doctoral research focused on visible-light-induced palladium catalysis and radical methods for site-selective C–H functionalization.

After my Ph.D., I joined Prof. Timothy Newhouse’s group at Yale University as a Postdoctoral Associate. In collaboration with Profs. Victor Batista and Robert Crabtree and Boehringer Ingelheim, I helped develop AI-assisted approaches to organic synthesis planning. This work drew on the collective knowledge encoded in language models trained on chemical reaction data to help guide experimental synthesis.

In 2026, I joined the Bakar Institute of Digital Materials for the Planet (BIDMaP) at UC Berkeley as a Postdoctoral Fellow, working with Prof. F. Dean Toste and Dr. Samuel Blau. My current research uses machine learning and atomistic foundation models to understand asymmetric catalysis, predict selectivity, and guide catalyst design for selective and sustainable chemical synthesis.

My long-term goal is to develop mechanistically informed, AI-assisted approaches that connect molecular modeling with experimental chemistry. By advancing synthetic and computational strategies together, I aim to address longstanding challenges in reaction discovery and asymmetric catalysis and enable more efficient synthesis of complex, biologically relevant molecules.
