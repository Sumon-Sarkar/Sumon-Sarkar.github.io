Sumon Sarkar, Ph.D.

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Bakar Institute of Digital Materials for the Planet (BIDMaP) at UC Berkeley, working with Prof. F. Dean Toste and Dr. Samuel Blau at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Associate in Prof. Timothy Newhouse’s laboratory at Yale University, where I integrated machine learning and data-driven approaches for synthesis planning in collaboration with Profs. Victor Batista and Robert Crabtree and with Boehringer Ingelheim.

My doctoral research with Prof. Vladimir Gevorgyan focused on visible-light-induced palladium catalysis and metal-free radical reactions for site-selective C–H functionalization of alcohols, amines, and alkenes.

Broadly, I am interested in integrating machine learning with synthetic organic chemistry to guide catalysis, reaction development, and late-stage functionalization. My long-term goal is to develop general, mechanistically informed strategies for longstanding synthetic challenges and to streamline access to complex, drug-like molecules.

Research program

Research interests

My research program spans three interconnected areas: the development of photocatalytic and radical methods for site-selective C–H functionalization; the application of machine learning to retrosynthetic planning and reaction prediction; and data-driven approaches to asymmetric catalyst optimization. These efforts are united by a commitment to experimental validation — computational predictions are tested at the bench, and bench observations motivate the next generation of models.

01

Photocatalytic C–H Functionalization & Radical Methodology

Achieving site-selective functionalization of unactivated C(sp3)–H bonds remains a central challenge in synthesis. Visible-light excitation of transition-metal intermediates provides access to open-shell pathways unavailable under thermal conditions. We demonstrated that photoexcitation of Pd–H species enhances hydride donor ability in the excited state, enabling anti-Markovnikov radical Heck-type reactivity and chemoselective head-to-tail alkene cross-dimerization.

Complementary metal-free strategies employing nitrogen-centered radicals and 1,5-HAT deliver site-selective β- and γ-C(sp3)–H borylation of aliphatic amines without transition metals. We further established that aryl triflates, conventionally limited to two-electron Pd(0)/Pd(II) oxidative addition, undergo single-electron reduction under visible light to generate aryl radicals competent for intramolecular C(sp3)–H arylation.

02

Machine Learning for Retrosynthetic Planning

The procedural knowledge required for reliable synthesis planning — choice of reagents, solvent, temperature, stoichiometry, and workup — is distributed across millions of published reports and remains largely inaccessible to computational methods. We developed MOSAIC, a mixture-of-experts large language-model framework in which 2,498 specialist models, each trained within a Voronoi-partitioned region of chemical reaction space, collectively generate complete and reproducible experimental protocols with associated confidence metrics.

Experimental validation across 35+ previously unreported compounds, spanning pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and functional materials, demonstrated a 71% overall success rate. Notably, MOSAIC proposed productive reaction conditions for transformations outside its direct training distribution, indicating capacity for meaningful extrapolation rather than mere interpolation.

03

Data-Driven Catalyst Design & Sustainable Synthesis

Catalyst and ligand optimization in asymmetric synthesis proceeds predominantly by empirical screening, which is resource-intensive and provides limited mechanistic insight into the origins of selectivity. At BIDMaP (Berkeley), in collaboration with Prof. F. Dean Toste and Dr. Samuel Blau (LBNL), I develop quantitative structure–enantioselectivity relationships and data-driven models that connect catalyst and substrate descriptors to experimental outcomes — yield, enantioselectivity, and turnover.

The objective is both predictive (identifying productive catalyst–substrate combinations prospectively) and interpretive (rationalizing selectivity-determining interactions in terms of accessible transition-state geometries and steric/electronic features).

A general radical strategy converts α-amino C–H bonds into C–B bonds under metal-free conditions, providing modular access to synthetically useful amine derivatives.
The problem. Visible-light palladium catalysis encompasses diverse reactivity modes whose mechanistic logic can be difficult to navigate. Contribution. This review organizes recent advances around photoexcited Pd intermediates, radical pathways, and the synthetic opportunities created by light-driven changes in palladium reactivity.
The problem. HAT can impart exceptional selectivity in C–H functionalization, but its relationship to downstream radical capture is often obscured. Contribution. A mechanistically focused review connects HAT design principles to synthetic applications, helping establish a framework for site-selective radical C–H functionalization.
The problem. Photoexcitation can fundamentally alter the redox and bond-forming properties of transition-metal intermediates. Contribution. This comprehensive review maps the mechanistic foundations and synthetic applications of visible-light-induced transition-metal catalysis across the field.
The problem. Aryl triflates typically engage in two-electron oxidative-addition chemistry rather than single-electron radical generation. Contribution. Visible-light palladium catalysis enables aryl-radical formation from aryl triflates, expanding these common electrophiles to intramolecular C(sp3)–H arylation.
The problem. Functionalizing remote, unactivated C(sp3)–H bonds of alcohols with alkene partners is a challenging selectivity problem. Contribution.
