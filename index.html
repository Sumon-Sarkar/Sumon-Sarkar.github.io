Sumon Sarkar, Ph.D.

I am a synthetic organic chemist working to make difficult molecular construction more predictable. My work combines new catalytic reactivity with mechanistic and computational tools for synthesis.

I am currently a BIDMaP Postdoctoral Fellow at UC Berkeley, where I work with Prof. F. Dean Toste and Dr. Samuel Blau on data-driven catalytic reaction discovery. Previously, I was a postdoctoral researcher at Yale University with Prof. Timothy R. Newhouse, where I worked on AI-assisted synthesis planning and practical retrosynthesis.

I received my Ph.D. from the University of Texas at Dallas with Prof. Vladimir Gevorgyan. My doctoral research developed photochemical and radical-relay methods for C–H functionalization, borylation, and palladium catalysis.

Research

New reactivity, then better rules for using it.

I study synthetic problems for which current reactions are insufficient: functionalizing unactivated C–H bonds, controlling reactive radical intermediates, and predicting which catalysts will deliver useful selectivity.

My earlier work explored excited-state Pd–H hydricity, radical C–H borylation, radical-relay Heck reactions, and aryl-radical generation from triflates. My current research asks how atomistic machine learning and mechanistic modeling can help design more sustainable catalytic transformations.

I also helped develop AI-guided synthesis-planning approaches that turn broad chemical knowledge into experimentally actionable routes. The goal is not to automate chemistry for its own sake, but to give chemists better starting points for making molecules that matter.
