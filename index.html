Synthetic chemistry · computation · discovery

Inventing reactions that do not exist yet—and making them predictable.

Sumon Sarkar develops new chemical reactivity for difficult molecular construction, then builds mechanistic and computational frameworks that reveal why it works and where it can go next.

Research program

Hard synthetic problems require both new reactivity and better ways to reason about it.

01

Radical C–H functionalization & photocatalysis

Many abundant C–H bonds remain chemically inert or selectivity-poor. I develop photochemical and radical-relay strategies that convert them into useful handles, including Pd–H excited-state hydricity, metal-free borylation, and aryl-radical chemistry from triflates.

02

AI-guided synthesis planning

Retrosynthesis is only useful when its proposals survive the bench. With MOSAIC, we developed a mixture-of-experts language-model framework designed to connect synthetic precedent with experimentally actionable routes.

03

Data-driven catalysis for sustainable synthesis

Predictive catalysis requires models that respect physical mechanism, conformational complexity, and sparse experimental data. At Berkeley, I combine atomistic ML, transition-state modeling, and experiment to make catalyst selection less empirical.
